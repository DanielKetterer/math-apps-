# Project-selection rubric

Score each dimension from 1 (low) to 5 (high). Always include a short rationale and key uncertainty. Do not let a high total override a serious mathematical or ethical concern.

| Dimension | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Educational value | Peripheral or vague outcome | Useful, bounded skill | Important concept or prerequisite with meaningful downstream value |
| Evidence of learner need | Pure guess | Plausible difficulty, limited evidence | Well-supported learner difficulty or clear unmet need |
| Interactive fit | Static explanation works equally well | Interaction adds some clarity | Manipulation, feedback, or dynamic representation is central to understanding |
| Reach / reuse | Narrow, one-off use | Several plausible contexts | Useful across multiple pathways, ages, or instructional settings |
| Feasibility | Unclear mathematics or large scope | Achievable with some uncertainty | Small, testable, standalone implementation |

## Suggested comparison

Use the five scores as a conversation starter. A simple unweighted total out of 25 is acceptable for an initial shortlist, but should not be mistaken for a validated measure. Evidence quality and mathematical correctness should be reviewed independently.

## Candidate patterns worth investigating

- A prerequisite with many important downstream dependents, especially when evidence suggests learners struggle with it.
- A transition between concrete, visual, tabular, graphical, and symbolic representations.
- A common incorrect strategy that can be made visible through interaction.
- A conceptual justification that a learner can explore by changing a mathematical object.
- A skill where several prerequisites converge and an app could help identify which part is missing.

Avoid making one app for every node. Prefer coherent learning objectives and reusable interactions over topic-count targets.
